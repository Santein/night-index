# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Players drawn to atmospheric mystery and psychological horror. They enter a short, replayable first-person experience, investigate an impossible television broadcast, and reconstruct a story by following teletext page numbers and making choices.

## Product Purpose

Create a 15–25 minute interactive horror story in which the television is both the primary interface and the unreliable narrator. A playthrough should feel complete, while repeated visits reveal new connections, secret pages, and substantially different endings.

## Brand Personality

Dreamlike, intimate, melancholic, quietly menacing. The tone draws from surreal small-town mystery, late-night broadcast ephemera, and domestic spaces that feel subtly wrong, without borrowing copyrighted characters, locations, dialogue, or plotlines.

## Anti-references

Avoid explicit gore, frequent jumpscares, generic haunted-house imagery, VHS-filter parody, cyberpunk neon, horror-game inventory clutter, and interfaces that place a conventional game HUD over the television.

## Design Principles

- The television is the world: every clue, choice, and consequence should feel native to the broadcast.
- Dread accumulates through repetition and changed details rather than sudden spectacle.
- Page numbers are both navigation and narrative evidence.
- Choices leave traces across branches so the story feels intertwined rather than reset between pages.
- The room reacts to the signal, turning interface state into environmental storytelling.
- Each story page balances one clear anchor, one invitation to act, and one unsettling shadow that remains unexplained.
- Important decisions reveal their immediate known cost when selected, require deliberate confirmation, and produce a visible consequence without exposing the full branch structure.
- Hidden pages are explicitly signposted. Manual tuning should feel intentional, never like guessing.
- The receiver-memory page is a diegetic recap: it echoes what the set remembers and offers a small number of suggestive paths when the player feels lost.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Support keyboard, mouse, and touch input; keep teletext text legible at common viewport sizes; never rely on color alone for choices; provide reduced-motion and reduced-flashing modes; expose meaningful controls to assistive technology. The game contains no spoken dialogue, so subtitles are not required. Important non-speech audio cues also receive optional visual indicators.
